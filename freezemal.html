FreezeMal: Unknown-Family Android Malware Detection Based on Multi-Task Learning

Xiaosong Chang1,2, Jinfu Chen1,2, Saihua Cai1,2, Wenjie Gu3, Sheng Wei1,2
1School of Computer Science and Communication Engineering, Jiangsu University, Jiangsu, China  |  2Jiangsu Key Laboratory of Security Technology for Industrial Cyberspace, Jiangsu University, Jiangsu, China  |  3Faculty of Computing and Data Science, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Hong Kong, China
Received: — Revised: — Accepted: — Academic Editor: — Guest Editor: —
Keywords: Android malware detection · unknown malware families · continual learning · incremental learning · multi-task learning
Machine learning methods are widely accepted as a panacea for Android malware detection. However, because existing methods overlook the relationships among malware families, they have an insufficient ability to detect unknown malware families. In this paper, we propose a multi-task learning (MTL) scheme to explicitly capture inter-family relationships and improve the generalization of malware detection. Specifically, we first construct a malicious behavior space via a multi-family detection task. Then, a clustering-based approach is applied to identify valuable samples for detection rule updating. Finally, a lightweight active learning strategy is introduced to dynamically optimize the detection strategy. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method can efficiently detect unknown malware families and significantly reduce the computational overhead in both the training and detection phases. Our method achieves an average F1 of 98.18% on the CICMalDroid2020 and VirusTotal datasets. Furthermore, it improves the F1 to 85.13% and 90.90%, respectively, in unknown malware family detection and concept drift settings, demonstrating its efficiency.
